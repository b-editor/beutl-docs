# 3D Scene Composition

Beutl allows you to create and compose 3D scenes alongside your 2D content. A 3D scene consists of a camera, lights, and 3D objects with materials.

## Overview

3D scenes in Beutl are composed of three main elements:

1. **Camera** - Defines the viewpoint
2. **Lights** - Illuminates the scene
3. **3D Objects** - The geometry and models in your scene

## Camera

The 3D camera in Beutl uses a perspective projection with the following properties:

- **Field of View** - The viewing angle (typically 45-90 degrees)
- **Near Plane** - The closest distance objects will be rendered
- **Far Plane** - The farthest distance objects will be rendered
- **Direction** - The direction the camera is facing
- **Up Vector** - The camera's up orientation

## Lights

Beutl supports three types of lights to illuminate your 3D scenes:

### Directional Light
Simulates distant light sources like the sun. Light rays are parallel.

### Point Light
Emits light in all directions from a single point in space, like a light bulb.

### Spot Light
Emits light in a cone shape from a point, like a flashlight or stage spotlight.

## 3D Objects

Beutl provides several built-in 3D object types:

- **Cube** - A basic box primitive
- **Sphere** - A spherical primitive
- **Plane** - A flat rectangular surface
- **3D Model** - Import external 3D model files

## Materials

Materials define how light interacts with 3D object surfaces. Beutl supports three material types:

### Basic Material

A simple material with classic Phong shading properties:

- **Diffuse** - The base color of the material
- **Ambient** - The color in shadowed areas
- **Specular** - The color of highlights
- **Shininess** - The size/intensity of specular highlights

### PBR Material (Physically Based Rendering)

A more realistic material system based on physical properties:

- **Albedo** - The base color map
- **Metallic** - How metallic the surface is (0 = dielectric, 1 = metal)
- **Roughness** - Surface roughness (0 = smooth/mirror-like, 1 = rough/matte)
- **AO (Ambient Occlusion)** - Shadows in crevices and corners
- **Emissive** - Self-illumination color
- **Normal Map** - Surface detail without additional geometry

### Transparent Material

A material that supports transparency and refraction effects.

## Shadow Settings

You can configure shadow rendering in your 3D scene:

- Enable/disable shadows per light source
- Adjust shadow quality and softness
- Control shadow distance and fade

## Best Practices

- Use directional lights for outdoor scenes
- Combine multiple light types for realistic indoor lighting
- Use PBR materials for the most realistic results
- Keep polygon counts reasonable for good performance
- Enable shadows selectively to balance quality and performance
